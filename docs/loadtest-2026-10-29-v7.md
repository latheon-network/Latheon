# Load and anonymity test, round 2: 27–29 October 2026 (Latheon test chain, V7 pools)

Round 1 (V6 pools, 200 wallets) is in [`loadtest-2026-10-27.md`](./loadtest-2026-10-27.md); method and caveats are the same.

**What it is.** 555 wallets used the test chain for about 45 hours: faucet claims, deposits into the fixed pools and through the router, withdrawals of whole deposits, of single notes and of part of an amount, through the relayer or paid by the wallet itself; 50 wallets were funded over the bridge from Arbitrum Sepolia and 19 bridged LTH back out. Up to 4 steps of different wallets ran at once. Each wallet followed one of four behaviours chosen by us: careful (waits 2–20 h, withdraws in parts or note by note to several fresh addresses through the relayer), typical (waits 20 min – 6 h, everything to one fresh address through the relayer), hasty (within 10 min, everything at once, through the relayer), self-paying (from the wallet that deposited). Afterwards an observer's simple heuristics were run on **public chain data only** and compared with the private journal.

**What it is not.** A simulation: the behaviours were chosen by us, so it measures heuristics against habits, not real users. The pools held almost only these test deposits. Code: `latheon-network/l2-eval-ci`, `testnet/loadtest/`. Wallet keys and the journal stay on the server.

**Run:** 3,018 steps, 0 failed in the end (fund 269, faucet 236, bridge-in 50, pool deposit 435, router deposit 666, withdraw all 620, withdraw notes 345, withdraw part 378, bridge-out 19). 4,329 deposits (notes) in the V7 pools, 1,986 withdrawal transactions. Proofs made again because a pool no longer knew their root: 0 (root history 100).

During the run: 5 withdrawals failed in the test script itself (a race when loading the tree's zero values right after a restart; fixed in l2-eval-ci `3307dfb`) and 1 because the node answered the relayer "nonce has already been used" (the relayer now retries with a fresh nonce, `cdf3495`). Nothing had been sent in either case; all 6 were run again and succeeded. The run was also paused for a while when container logs had filled the server disk to 91 % (log rotation added, `bb01802`).

## What it says

- A bigger crowd helps everyone, even careless users: with roughly 2.5 times the anonymity set, the share of hasty and typical withdrawals that simple heuristics could link fell by about 40 %.
- Paying the withdrawal from the depositing wallet still links it every time; the crowd does not help there. Use the relayer and a fresh address.
- The careful behaviour was again not linked by any of these heuristics. Stronger analysis (timing distributions, cross-pool patterns) was not attempted.
