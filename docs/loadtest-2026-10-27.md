# Load and anonymity test, 26–27 October 2026 (Latheon test chain, V6 pools)

**What it is.** 200 wallets used the test chain for about 21 hours the way people might: faucet claims, deposits into the fixed pools and through the router, withdrawals of whole deposits, of single notes and of part of an amount, through the relayer or paid by the wallet itself; 25 wallets were funded over the bridge from Arbitrum Sepolia and 11 bridged LTH back out. Each wallet followed one of four behaviours chosen by us (hasty, typical, self-paying, careful). Afterwards an observer's simple heuristics were run on **public chain data only** and compared with the private journal of what really happened.

**What it is not.** A simulation: the behaviours were chosen by us, so it shows how far simple heuristics get against typical habits, not how real users behave. The pools held only these test deposits. The test ran on the V6 pools, which share the pre-V7 circuit flaw (THREAT-MODEL §8.0); the flaw does not change these results, which concern linkability, not soundness. Code: `latheon-network/l2-eval-ci`, `testnet/loadtest/`. The wallet keys and the journal stay on the server.

**Run:** 1,114 steps, 0 failed (fund 77, faucet 98, bridge-in 25, pool deposit 163, router deposit 243, withdraw all 229, withdraw notes 123, withdraw part 145, bridge-out 11). 1,655 deposits, 748 withdrawal transactions. Proofs made again because a pool no longer knew their root: 0 (root history 100).

## What it says

- Paying the withdrawal from the depositing wallet links it every time. Use the relayer and a fresh address.
- Withdrawing a distinctive amount soon after depositing it is the next biggest leak (67 % precision). Waiting and withdrawing in standard shapes removes it.
- "The newest deposit is the one" is a poor guess (5 % precision) once a pool has traffic.
- The careful behaviour (wait 2–20 hours, withdraw in parts or note by note to several fresh addresses through the relayer) was not linked by any of these heuristics. Stronger analysis (timing distributions, cross-pool patterns) was not attempted.
