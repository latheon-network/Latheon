What changed recently

Dated milestones, each confirmed by transactions. Full history on the roadmap.

  1. 14 Nov 2026Arbitrum’s own bridge works with the test chain

    Added in the settings of bridge.arbitrum.io (config on the developers page), it moved 1 LTH from Arbitrum Sepolia to the test chain in seconds and 1 LTH back, claimed about four hours later. The faucet now gives 300 LTH per address a day.

  2. 12 Nov 2026A withdrawal without our RPC or relayer

    A pool withdrawal was sent through the test chain inbox on Arbitrum Sepolia and executed on the test chain, so a refusal by our RPC or relayer cannot stop one. Forcing a transaction in with the sequencer stopped takes 96 hours and is not tested; leaving the chain still depends on our validator.

  3. 11 Nov 2026Delayed withdrawals, and reads that name no note

    The relayer can send a withdrawal at a random moment within up to 24 hours, so the time of your visit and the time of the withdrawal differ; it still sees when you asked. The demo and the SDK no longer ask the node about single notes. Both checked with live transactions.

  4. 7 Nov 2026Payouts to a list of recipients

    Pay salaries or vendors from the pools: one deposit session, then each recipient is paid through the relayer, and one report shows the accountant which withdrawal went to whom. Two live runs with three recipients each. Amounts stay public.

  5. 6 Nov 2026SDK run on the test chain

    The JavaScript SDK for V7 made router deposits and withdrawals on the test chain, including a three-note batch through the relayer. Not on npm yet.

  6. 1 Nov 2026Disclosure report for an accountant or auditor

    Prove many deposits at once: one file with a proof per note, made for the auditor's one-time code; the auditor tab checks it for free and exports CSV. Optionally, where withdrawn notes went.

Honest note: the pools hold mostly our own test deposits, so the crowd you hide in is close to empty. The mechanics work and can be checked on-chain; real privacy needs real users.

Honest gas note: measured on our test chain with V7 (27 October 2026), a router deposit of 2 notes used 1,749,602 gas for execution and 12,219,550 in total; a withdrawal of both notes with a relayer fee, 625,896 and 35,912,159. Most of the total is the charge for publishing data to the parent chain (Arbitrum Sepolia), so it depends on that chain, not on our contracts. Details in STATUS.md.

All deployments →

Security note: an internal review found a flaw in every pre-V7 circuit — a crafted proof could withdraw without a deposit. We confirmed it with a test and fixed it; the current pools are V7 (withdraw_v5). The older pools (V3–V6, and the pools on the three external testnets) are frozen for deposits in our interface and kept only so existing notes can be withdrawn. Their contracts cannot be paused and still accept deposits sent by other apps: do not deposit into them. Testnet only — no real funds were ever at risk. Details in STATUS.md and THREAT-MODEL.md.

Founder

Who builds Latheon

Vladimir Smirnov, founder of Latheon

Crypto lacks privacy. When every transaction is public, people and organizations can be exposed, and their data can end up in the hands of scammers. I knew a way to reduce that risk, so I started building it.

Latheon is a privacy protocol on Ethereum, built and maintained by me alone. It is live on testnets only, and the long-term goal is a Layer 2 where privacy is the default. Before this I worked as a lead auditor in a control and audit department and studied public administration. That background shaped my approach: privacy should hold up under verification. The code and tests are open, the numbers are measured, and I say plainly what is not done yet, including that there is no independent audit.
